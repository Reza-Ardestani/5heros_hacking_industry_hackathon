# Customer discovery and pilot plan

Prepared October 3, 2026; no interviews or outreach completed. This guide is for
the team to use with willing participants. Do not record identifiable or
confidential study material without permission.

## Weekend discovery, proposed three-hour budget

Assign one team member to discovery and one to evidence/pitch edits. Keep the
remaining implementation work bounded while the problem is tested. Roles are
suggestions, not assigned ownership. Seek a traffic engineer/consultant, a
technical lead, and a program/budget reviewer; unrelated acquaintances do not
substitute for these roles. If access is unavailable, label customer validation
pending in the pitch.

1. **First 30 minutes:** identify the exact decision, willing participants, and
   a public or shareable completed study. Use the interview below before showing
   the app. Avoid pitching a solution in the problem interview.
2. **Next 90 minutes:** three approximately 20-minute conversations plus notes.
   Ask about the latest real task, not a hypothetical willingness to use AI.
3. **Final 60 minutes:** compare findings, update claim statuses, choose one
   target workflow, then show the prototype for a separate usability observation.
   Three interviews are directional discovery, not market validation.

## Non-leading interview questions

1. What decisions do you own? Walk us through the most recent corridor study or
   renewal funding decision, from trigger to approval.
2. What data, models and tools did you actually use? What could you show us
   without exposing confidential information?
3. Which step took the most effort? How often does it recur? What was the
   consequence: extra analyst work, delayed review, poor evidence, or something else?
4. How were alternatives chosen and compared? What required another run?
   What does your existing software already automate well?
5. How were costs estimated, constraints enforced, and rejected options explained?
6. Who reviewed the result? What questions or corrections came back?
7. When was faster evidence valuable? What made you trust or reject a model?
8. Which outcomes besides delay mattered? Ask openly before suggesting modes,
   safety, accessibility, reliability or asset condition.
9. If the current process were improved, how would you measure success?
10. Who could authorize a pilot and purchase? What access/procurement restrictions apply?

Do not ask “Would our AI solve your problem?” or invite agreement that the city
chooses randomly. Ask for concrete incidents, artifacts and counterexamples.

## Observation after the problem interview

Give a practitioner the prototype's synthetic scenario and ask them to state
the decision, set limits, run the comparison, explain a rejected alternative,
and locate the exported assumptions. Observe without coaching. Record wrong
interpretations and time to a correct explanation. A favorable reaction is
usability evidence, not proof of field efficacy or purchasing intent.

## Evidence record template

For each conversation record a consented anonymized participant ID, role, date,
decision type, actual toolchain, described episode, supporting artifact, effort
estimate and its basis, consequences, contradictory evidence, and follow-up.
Separate direct quotations, paraphrases and team interpretation. Store private
notes outside the public repo; publish only an approved aggregate summary.
Do not fabricate interview quotations or retroactively label this plan as findings.

## Continue, narrow or redirect

- **Continue the corridor workflow hypothesis** if at least two relevant
  practitioners independently describe recurring comparison/review effort,
  identify a specific gap despite existing tools, and a lead can supply a pilot
  case. This is a team screening rule, not statistical proof.
- **Narrow** if pain is only report preparation or model import; test that task
  rather than promise a better optimization engine.
- **Redirect through a separate design/spec** if the real job is maintenance
  portfolio prioritization. Preserve this demo; do not relabel traffic delay as
  asset risk or rewrite the weekend app around uncollected condition data.
- **Stop the unsupported differentiation claim** if existing tools satisfy the
  job and no recurring gap is found. Keep the demonstrator as technical evidence.

## Pilot beyond the weekend

Agree one corridor, accepted input/model formats, observations, cost scope,
engineering objectives and tolerances. Reproduce the accepted current plan
before testing alternatives. Add actual movements/modes and local constraints,
use an independently reviewed baseline, then freeze proposed alternatives for
evaluation on additional observations/seeds. Compare deliverable quality and
work time against the customer's usual process. Qualified approval precedes any
field test; road-effect claims require an agreed field evaluation design.
