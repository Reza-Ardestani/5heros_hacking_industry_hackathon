# ICP-BB-002 — Dashboard client

Status: target hypothesis; no practitioner interview, City partnership, purchasing
authority, or willingness to pay confirmed. [Journey UJ-BB-002](../../User_Journey_2/journey.md).

Primary user: a municipal mobility analyst or consultant analyst reviewing where
reported incidents, closures, and study context warrant attention. An operations
lead or transportation engineer reviews the shortlist. The initial interface is
desktop-first; this is not a consumer navigation or emergency dispatch app.

Job: when reviewing a corridor or an incident pattern, help me inspect current and
historical evidence, see coverage and timestamps, and hand off a bounded study
without copying filters and sources manually.

Daily pains to validate: repeated searches across feeds, disconnected historical
and current views, stale data treated as live, and loss of study context in handoffs.
Existing dashboards may already cover these needs; no claim that the City lacks
tools or makes decisions randomly. Validate a concrete task and its existing tools.

Buying roles: mobility program owner or consulting principal, through their actual
procurement process. Likely contributors: GIS/data analyst and qualified engineer.
Travelers benefit from better decisions but are not the initial software buyers.

Pilot fit: a reviewable incident/closure workflow, shared source permissions,
defined geography and reporting period, and an analyst who can assess whether a
shortlist is useful. Measure correct handoff time, freshness mistakes, and source
traceability; dashboard visits alone do not establish value.

Boundary: incident concentration is not a measurement of congestion, delay, crash
probability, or causality. Forecasts describe the recorded reporting process under
their model assumptions; sparse coverage and publishing behavior matter.
